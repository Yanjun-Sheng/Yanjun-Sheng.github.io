Research

I study near-field cosmology: I use the dynamics of the Milky Way's stellar halo to weigh our Galaxy and the Large Magellanic Cloud, and to reconstruct how they came together.

  • 01
    The Milky Way–LMC interaction
    The LMC is massive enough to pull the Milky Way's halo and disc out of equilibrium. I model these perturbations and use them to test whether the LMC is on its first infall (it is) and to constrain the masses and orbits of both galaxies from the kinematics of halo tracers.
  • 02
    N-body simulations — the HaloDance suite
    I built a suite of ~2,800 tailored N-body simulations of the MW–LMC system and used it to forecast how precisely Gaia astrometry, combined with spectroscopy, can measure the key parameters — reaching fractional uncertainties of roughly 11–25%.
  • 03
    Simulation-based inference
    I connect simulations to data with simulation-based inference — training statistical machine-learning models (neural networks, normalizing flows) to infer parameters without an explicit likelihood — and I quantify how much information is lost when moving from full field-level inference to compressed summary statistics.

Other directions

I also work at the edges of this program: tidal stellar streams as cold dynamical probes of the Galactic potential (with modern tools like flow matching and score-based inference), and galactic archaeology with large spectroscopic surveys such as SDSS-V — including the data-compression and deep-learning pipelines that make inference from millions of spectra tractable.
