About

I'm a PhD candidate at the Research School of Astronomy & Astrophysics, ANU, working with Yuan-Sen Ting on the dynamics of the Milky Way's stellar halo and its interaction with the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC).

My field is near-field cosmology — using the positions, motions, and chemistry of nearby stars as a fossil record to reconstruct how our Galaxy formed and grew. The Milky Way's outer halo is an especially sensitive recorder: it is still ringing from the recent, massive infall of the LMC.

In my first paper I used the kinematics of about 1,100 RR Lyrae stars to show that the LMC is on its first infall into the Milky Way and must be surprisingly massive. To model that signal in detail, I built HaloDance, a suite of ~2,800 tailored N-body simulations — direct gravitational simulations that follow millions of particles forward in time — and forecast how well upcoming Gaia and spectroscopic data can pin down the masses and shapes of both galaxies. Most recently, I connect these simulations to data with simulation-based inference (SBI): instead of writing down an explicit likelihood, I train statistical machine-learning models (neural networks and normalizing flows) on the simulations to infer physical parameters directly, and I compare full field-level inference against compressed summary statistics.

Also interested in

Beyond the Milky Way–LMC system, two directions I keep coming back to:

  • ›
    Tidal stellar streams as dynamical probes
    Thin, cold streams like GD-1 trace the Galactic gravitational field. Modern tools — from flow matching to score-based inference — can jointly recover the potential and a stream's progenitor, giving a complementary handle on the dark-matter distribution.
  • ›
    Galactic archaeology with large spectroscopic surveys
    Reconstructing the Milky Way's accretion and merger history from surveys such as SDSS-V / Milky Way Mapper, and building the data-compression and deep-learning pipelines needed to turn millions of stellar spectra into robust parameters and constraints.
